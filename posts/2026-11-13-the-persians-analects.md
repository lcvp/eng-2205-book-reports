## Aeschylus, The Persians

They must have known they are in the wrong, as they are the invaders.

### First reading - Follow the situation

> Who is Atossa?

Persian queen, mother of Xerxes.

> Who is the Messenger?

A survivor of the war.

> What news does he bring?

Persians lost, suffering, death.

> What happened to the Persian forces?

Lost. Most of them died, including the generals. Xerxes survived, though.

> How do the Persian characters respond?

With grief. No anger.

### Second reading - Mark three things

#### Grief

> Is the defeated enemy presented simply as "the enemy," or as individual human beings who suffer?

Weird question, as obviously not, this is even mentioned in the guide itself. Though most named people are generals, not your average foot soldiers.

#### Power / Leadership

> Does military power guarantee good leadership?

There is zero corrolation between the two. So no.

> How does the Messenger explain the disaster?

In regards to the current heading, partly by recounting how Xerxes' plan lead many men to their deaths.

#### Voice / Perspective

> Whose suffering do we hear?

We hear **about** the Persians' sufferings, though we hear the Messenger's, the queen and the chorus' laments.

> Whose actions are praised?

The person around line 170. I am unsure if this is referring to a person, or a group of people. It could be referring to the generals (valour of our choice nobility)? The English is weird.

> Whose point of view seems to control the scene?

The messenger's.

## Confucius, The Analects

### First reading - notice the form

> Who asks the question?

Mostly the disciples?

> What situation produces the answer?

I don't understand this question. When the person answers them?

> Is Confucius giving a rule, an example, a correction, or a challenge?

Most of the time, he gives these comparisons that I find to be smirkish.

### Second reading - Track four problems

#### Learning and self-cultivation

> What does it mean to learn?

To absord knowledge within oneself.

> Is knowledge enough?

This question is bad. Enough for what? For exams? Sure. But i'm guessing in the context of the text, it is not enough to be a well-rounded person with knowledge alone.

> What is the relationship between learning and practice?

You reinforce your learning by practicing.

> Why might Confucius care about what a person does rather than only what a person says?

Word is cheap. What people say isn't necessarily what they actually thinks.

#### Ritual, behavior, and character

> Is ritual only ceremony?

In a sense, yes.

> Can repeated forms of behavior shape a person's character?

Absolutely. I'm pretty sure there are studies that confirmed this.

> Is behaving correctly the same as being genuinely good?

No. Those who stand at the top determine what's "wrong" and what's "right".

#### Government, trust, and morale example

> What makes political authority legitimate?

"Legitimacy" is vague. In his time, power was mostly originally attained by force.

> What matters more: force or trust?

In terms of long term health and happiness of the people, trust. In terms of short term gratification for the ruler: force.

> Should rulers control people through punishment, or influence them through example?

Both. They are not mutually exclusive. Crimes that are unpunished are examples themselves.

> What responsibilities belong to the ruler himself?

To govern.

#### Tradition, language, and change

> What does it mean to "transmit" a tradition?

In a vague sense, to make one's own traditions practiced by groups not originally practiced that tradition.

> Why does language matter for social order?

Because in ancient China (and right now, also, even in Vietnamese), language contains honorifics that signals respects and seniority/juniority.

> Can an old tradition respond to a changing world?

It heavily depends on the tradition.

> Should a thoughtful person try to reform a damaged society- or withdraw from it?

It depends on the severity of the damage. If the damage is still in the realm of possible reformation, then they should. Otherwise, in severe cases like that of the cyberpunk genre: absolutely not.
